# Creative Ideation

Use this skill when building new things, exploring system architecture, or looking for non-obvious ways to solve a problem without defaulting to textbook patterns.

## When to use

- Brainstorming a greenfield feature, tool, or architecture.
- Looking for fresh, creative angles before committing to a design.
- Avoiding standard, bloated, or overly predictable implementations.

Do not use for straightforward bug fixes or tasks where the design is already determined.

## Protocol

### 1. Drain the 3 obvious ways
State the 3 most direct, conventional ways to build this. Keep descriptions to one sentence each. These exist solely to get the baseline out of the way.

### 2. Generate the next 10 distinct ways
List 10 completely different ways to build or solve this.

Rules for these 10:
- Do not repeat or tweak the first 3 baseline ideas.
- Each idea must attack the problem from a genuinely different angle or challenge an unspoken assumption.
- Keep descriptions short (1 to 2 sentences per idea).
- Do not filter out unconventional ideas, let the range be wide.

### 3. Surface the next 5 downstream build levers
List 5 second-order questions, non-obvious trade-offs, or downstream opportunities that emerged from the 10 ideas.

### 4. Checkpoint
Ask the user which directions look interesting before drafting any plan or writing code.
